The nine candidate categories in the original brief largely hold, but two need splitting. "Data brokers and marketplaces" is not one category in Canada; it is two economically unrelated businesses, legacy consumer data brokers whose buyer is an advertiser versus AI-training dataset marketplaces whose buyer is a model lab, and Canada has real depth in the first and almost nothing in the second. Synthetic data likewise splits cleanly into privacy-preserving record synthesis and simulation or world-model synthesis, which share a name and share nothing else. Published commercial market maps are cruder: the most common vendor framing is a four-bucket one of marketplaces, labelling services, custom sourcing partners and synthetic data generators [V, forage.ai, 2026, itself a vendor in the category]. Conventional analyst segmentation cuts by modality and end-use industry, which is useless for a market map because a labelling shop and a synthetic-data vendor both land under "healthcare, text" [I].

The useful discriminator is the unit of value charged, because it differs sharply by category and it determines who the buyer is.

Categories A and B are converging at the vendor level and should not be treated as competing firms: Scale AI Inc., Surge AI, Mercor and Turing all appear as both "data giants" in the RL-environment taxonomy and as human-data vendors, and TELUS Digital markets both bulk annotation and 20+ expert domains off one contributor pool [I, from RL List and telusdigital.com].

The three clean negatives are C, B and D2. The RL-environment negative is the most defensible finding in this research, because it rests on an industry-maintained directory sweeping the whole vendor population and finding roughly 28 companies in the San Francisco Bay Area, 7 in New York, plus Gray Swan AI in Pittsburgh, Huzzle Labs in London and Andromede in Lausanne, and zero Canadian headquarters [V, RL List 2026]. The same sweep noted that some of those teams have University of Waterloo alumni, which makes this a talent-export finding rather than a market-presence finding [V, same source; I on the interpretation]. For category B, the named players across every source reached are Scale AI Inc., Surge AI, Mercor, Turing and Centific, all US, and the contributor-side platforms Canadians can join are Mercor, DataAnnotation.tech, Outlier and Alignerr, all US-operated [V, Troveo; aigigjobs.com]. For D2, the named marketplace platforms are AWS Data Exchange, Databricks Marketplace, Scale AI Inc. and Hugging Face, plus Troveo (US) and Human Native AI (UK) [V, marketintelo; Troveo].

There is a fourth, near-negative: collective rights licensing. Access Copyright is Canada's copyright collective for published text works, collecting from businesses, government, schools and libraries [V, accesscopyright.ca]. No evidence was found that it has launched an AI training licence, while the US Copyright Clearance Center launched a collective AI licence in July 2024 [V, Publishing Perspectives] [U on whether an Access Copyright product exists and was simply not found; three sources were checked and none surfaced one].

The reason categories B and C are empty in Canada is the same reason, and it is structural rather than accidental. Winning in the environment market means being embedded in a lab's research loop: "Frontier labs gravitate toward teams they trust and treat as extensions of their own research organizations, and winning teams will look like thought partners embedded with frontier labs rather than traditional tooling vendors" [V, Wing VC]. There is no frontier lab in Canada of the relevant scale to embed inside, and the nearest analogue, Cohere, is a buyer rather than a seller in this market, and even that is unestablished (see the demand section).

One caution on sizing before moving on. Anthropic was reported to have discussed spending over US$1 billion on RL environments over the following year [V that this was reported, The Information via Epoch AI, Sept 2025 report cited Jan 2026] [U on the underlying figure; it is a second-hand characterisation of private spend, and the original report is roughly 12 months old, so treat as STALE]. Set against a measured total AI training-dataset market of US$3.19B in 2025 rising to US$3.87B in 2026 [V, The Business Research Company], a single lab's environment budget would be a material fraction of the entire measured market. Either the market sizings exclude RL environments or they are badly stale [I]. Do not reconcile these figures; the discrepancy is itself the finding.
