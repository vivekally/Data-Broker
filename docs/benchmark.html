Data vendors for LLM, world-model and robotics labs

An independent scorecard of 45 companies that sell data, environments and evaluations to frontier labs: the human-data foundries that feed LLM post-training, the video and simulation suppliers that feed world models, and the teleoperation and motion-capture suppliers that feed robot foundation models. Each is scored 1–10 on 13 dimensions, with every fact behind the scores tagged by evidence strength.

Three buyers, almost no overlap

Setting each vendor's fit for the three buyers side by side shows how little they share. The LLM suppliers have no bar for the other two. The physical-AI suppliers overlap more, because robot data helps action-conditioned world models and simulation serves both, but they are led by different firms: licensed-video and simulation vendors for world models, teleoperation and motion-capture vendors for robotics. Only two vendors clear 6 for all three buyers: TELUS Digital, a managed-annotation incumbent, and Encord, a data platform. Neither leads any of them.

What the benchmark shows

  • No vendor leads more than one market well, except at the physical-AI end. The top LLM-fit scores belong to Surge, Snorkel, Mercor and Handshake. The top world-model scores belong to Lightwheel, Troveo and XDOF; the top robotics scores to XDOF, Lightwheel, Encord and Mecka AI. None of the LLM leaders publicly sells robot demonstration data at scale, and none of the robotics-data leaders runs an expert network for text post-training. micro1 and Scale are the clearest attempts to straddle LLM and robotics, and both score below the robotics specialists. [I, from the capability columns]
  • Ownership is now a buyer gate, not a footnote. Scale AI has the deepest capability set in the table and the lowest neutrality score. After Meta bought 49% of it, Google, OpenAI and xAI cut or reduced work with it within weeks, and the business moved to Mercor and Surge. [V, analyst write-ups of the June 2025 deal, as carried in the Canada brief] The same logic now applies to strategic money in robotics data: Mecka AI's 2026 round includes NVIDIA, Samsung and Microsoft's M12, all potential buyers. [V, Dealroom, 7 Oct 2026]
  • Security and labor are the weakest columns in the industry, and growth makes them worse. Mercor, one of the two fastest-growing firms in the table, lost Meta's work after a March 2026 breach that reached contractor identity documents and biometrics, and faces several class actions over it. [V, TechCrunch, 9 Apr 2026; SIA] Scale and Surge both face misclassification suits from contributors. A contributor pool of tens of thousands of people holding lab task data is an attack surface that labs are only starting to audit. [I]
  • Headline revenue is gross, and roughly two thirds of it is contractor pay. Mercor's contractors receive 60–70% of top-line revenue, and Handshake's AI-training business nets about $300M on nearly $1B gross. [V, Sacra; Dealroom, Apr 2026] Comparing these firms on gross run-rate against software companies overstates them by about three times. [I]
  • World models and robotics are adjacent markets, not one. Lightwheel and XDOF sit near the top of both, because each pairs robot data with simulation. Below them the lists divide: Troveo and Shutterstock rank high for world-model labs but low or not at all for robotics, since unlabelled video without actions is of limited use for training a robot policy; XDOF and Mecka AI rank higher for robotics than for world models. [I, from the fit columns]
  • Robotics data comes from three supply models, and none is safe from substitutes. Teleoperation on real robots (XDOF, Microagi, Cortex AI) is the scarcest and most robot-specific. Wearable capture of people doing real work (Mecka AI, Human Archive, Build AI, Luel) scales fastest, and it is where questions about pay and consent concentrate. Studio motion capture (Bones Studio, Noitom) is moving to open research releases with paid enterprise tiers. [I, from the vendor evidence] Each competes with a non-vendor source: state-funded training centres in China, such as Beijing's 10,000-square-metre humanoid centre [V, Beijing government, Oct 2025], and labs collecting in-house. XDOF's own pitch is that a lab would need a warehouse of robots to match it. [V that the argument was made, TechCrunch, 17 Jun 2026]
  • 2026's money is moving to robotics data. XDOF launched with $70M in June and was reportedly in Series B talks at $1.2B three months later; Microagi raised Germany's largest-ever seed, $55M, ten months after founding; Mecka AI raised a $60M Series B led by Sequoia in October; Lightwheel booked about 550M yuan of orders in Q1 alone. [V, SiliconANGLE; Sifted; Dealroom; Gasgoo] [U on the XDOF Series B, summary only]
  • Raw egocentric video is commoditising; action-labelled and teleoperated data is not. Build AI released roughly a million hours of factory egocentric video under Apache 2.0 in April 2026, which sets a price of zero for unlabelled first-person footage. [V that the release was reported, Tesseract; U on the hour count] The firms commanding rounds are the ones selling robot-specific teleoperation, motion capture and simulation loops, where open data does not substitute. [I]
  • Rights is the one dimension where licensors beat the foundries. Licensed-content suppliers (Shutterstock, Protege, Troveo, Wirestock, Cloudflare's Human Native) score highest on chain of title because their unit of sale is the licence itself. For world-model labs training on video, that matters more than it does in text post-training, where the data is commissioned rather than found. [I]
  • One result may touch the Canada brief. Dealroom describes Mecka AI as a New York and Toronto startup with $128M disclosed across three rounds since August 2025. [V, Dealroom, 7 Oct 2026] Fortune's profile places its headquarters in New York and mentions no Canadian office. [V, Fortune, 1 Jun 2026] The Canada brief found no Canadian pure-play AI data-supply company with a venture round as of 2026-09-30. If Mecka AI has a real Toronto operation, it would qualify that finding; on current evidence it is most likely a US company with at most a Canadian team. [U] The brief is reproduced verbatim and has not been edited; this is flagged for its next revision.

Methodology

Who is in it. Companies whose main business is supplying training data, RL environments or evaluations to model developers, sorted into seven types. First-party content owners that license their own archives (Reddit, news publishers) are out of scope, as are labs' in-house data teams. Vendors were drawn from the two briefs on this site, the RL List and Pavlov's List directories they cite, and 2026 funding coverage. Robotics vendors were added in a second sweep of 2026 teleoperation, wearable-capture and motion-capture funding news; one egocentric vendor whose only public evidence was its own marketing was left out. Inclusion is not endorsement, and exclusion is not a judgment: a vendor with too little public evidence to score was left out rather than guessed at.

How the scores are set. Each dimension has written anchors for 1–3, 4–6, 7–8 and 9–10, shown in the cards below. A score is the anchor band the public evidence supports, placed within the band by the strength of that evidence. Self-reported claims can lift a score by at most one band over what third-party evidence supports. A dash means either the vendor does not sell that capability, or nothing public was found to score it; the Scored column shows how many of the 13 a vendor was scored on.

The composites. Labs buy data per work order, often from a specialist, so a vendor should not need to be broad to be useful. Each buyer fit is therefore built from the vendor's strongest capabilities plus the gates every lab applies:

  • Capability = 0.7 × lead score + 0.3 × the best remaining score in that buyer's capability group. Unscored capabilities count as zero. The lead must come from what that buyer cannot do without:
    • LLM labs: lead from expert depth, RL environments or evals; throughput can support but not lead, because the evidence in both briefs is that labs now buy scarce expertise rather than volume.
    • World-model labs: lead from video corpus or simulation, the pretraining inputs for models that predict how scenes evolve; robot data can support.
    • Robotics labs: lead from robot and embodied data or simulation, the inputs for policies that act; video can support but not lead, because footage without actions or forces does not train a policy on its own.
  • Gates = mean of the scored buyer-gate dimensions: rights, security, neutrality, traction. These are the four gates the Canada brief found labs apply to every supplier, in the buyer's own procurement language.
  • LLM fit, World-model fit and Robotics fit = 0.6 × capability + 0.4 × gates. A vendor with no capability in a group gets no fit score for that buyer.
  • Overall = plain mean of every scored dimension, as in the benchmark this page is modelled on. It rewards narrow vendors scored on few dimensions, which is why the buyer views sort by fit instead.

Evidence grade. A core figures come from filings, a company press release confirmed by an independent outlet, or several independent reports that agree. B core figures rest on one reputable outlet, an analyst estimate, or company claims. C little beyond the vendor's own site or directory listings. The grade measures how much to trust the row, not how good the vendor is.

Limits

  • Nothing here was tested hands-on. Unlike consumer software, a data vendor cannot be trialled without a contract, and quality varies by project and by the team assigned. A low score is a statement about public evidence, not about a vendor's delivered quality on any given engagement.
  • Almost every revenue figure is a run-rate, gross of contributor pay, and self-reported or analyst-estimated. Where sources conflict the conflict is stated in the vendor's evidence, not averaged.
  • This market moves weekly. Rounds reported as “in talks” are tagged as such and not treated as closed. Several 2026 valuations in circulation (AfterQuery at $3.2B, XDOF at $1.2B, Mercor at $20B) were unconfirmed at the as-of date.
  • Customer names are almost never public. Labs bind vendors to confidentiality, so traction scores lean on revenue scale and credible reporting rather than named logos. Claims such as “four of the five top labs” are tagged [U] unless independently confirmed.
  • Conduct scores cover only what is on the public record. A vendor with no reported incident is not shown to have a clean record; it shows a dash.
  • Corrections are welcome. Open an issue on the repository with a source. Scores will move when the evidence does, and the version number will change with them.
